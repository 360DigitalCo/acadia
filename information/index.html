How to read Acadia

A quick guide to the colors, labels, numbers, and buttons in search results. The colors identify what a piece of information means. They do not grade a paper or tell you whether its claims are true.

Color key

Blue: paper title

Open the publisher page, repository record, or indexed version of the paper.

Green: authors

Names of the people listed as authors. A clickable name opens the indexed author profile when one is available.

Purple: journal or source

Where the work is listed, such as a journal, conference, publisher, or repository. Check the original record for publication status.

Orange: year and citation count

The reported publication year and how many works this index says cite the paper. A high number is not a quality score.

Teal: publication type

The kind of record, such as article, review, book, dataset, thesis, conference paper, or preprint.

Green badge: Open access

An index reports that a free-to-read copy is available. It may be a repository manuscript rather than the publisher-formatted paper.

Gold badge: Preprint

A manuscript shared before or outside the final peer-reviewed journal version. Look for a published version before citing it.

Violet: DOI

A persistent identifier used to find a work even if the publisher changes its web address.

Source-context meter

A heuristic based on source-domain and metadata hints. It is not a paper-quality grade, peer-review guarantee, or fact-check.

What the result buttons do

DetailsOpens the metadata Acadia has for a work, including identifiers, abstract, source, versions, and available links.
SaveAdds the record to My library in this browser. Saved records are not synced to an account or another device.
CiteCreates a formatted reference in APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE, BibTeX, or RIS. Always check the generated citation against the original record.
Show abstractExpands or collapses a short summary of the study. An abstract is not the full paper; a missing abstract in Acadia does not mean the paper has none.
Cited by NOpens indexed works that cite this item when citation links are available. Counts and link lists may be incomplete or different in other databases.
Related articlesShows records the index considers related. Similarity does not mean the papers agree, are equally reliable, or cite each other.
All versionsLists alternate publisher, repository, and manuscript copies associated with the same work. Check dates and versions before using a copy.
PDF / Open accessOpens a free full-text link supplied by an index, if one is available. Access can change and the version may be a preprint or accepted manuscript.

Research terms in plain English

DOIDigital Object Identifier. A stable identifier, often shown like 10.1234/example.56. Visiting https://doi.org/... usually forwards you to the current record. A DOI does not prove the paper is correct or peer-reviewed.
AbstractA short explanation of a paper's question, methods, results, and sometimes limitations. It is a summary, not a replacement for reading the study.
MetadataFacts describing a work: title, authors, publication, date, DOI, source links, and citations. Metadata is submitted by different organizations and can be missing or inaccurate.
CitationsOther works an index has identified as referring to this work. Databases count different material and match references differently, so the totals often disagree.
Peer reviewA review process where other experts assess a research submission before publication. A journal label alone does not guarantee that a particular version has passed peer review.
PreprintA manuscript made public before formal journal peer review or outside the final published version. It can still be useful, but findings may change.
Open accessA work or version that can be read without a subscription under the available access terms. Free-to-read does not automatically mean free to reuse.
h-indexAn author-level metric: an h-index of 8 means the indexed record has at least 8 works cited at least 8 times each. It depends strongly on field, career length, and database coverage.
i10-indexThe number of indexed works that have at least 10 citations. Different indexes may show different values.
Journal / publisher / repositoryA journal publishes scholarly articles; a publisher distributes them; a repository hosts copies such as preprints or accepted manuscripts. Some records may appear in more than one place.

Search and filter tools

ArticlesSearch scholarly records from OpenAlex, Crossref, and Semantic Scholar. These are bibliographic indexes, not every web page or paper ever published.
Case lawOpens specialist legal-search services for court opinions. Confirm the jurisdiction, court, date, and whether a decision is still good law before relying on it.
PatentsOpens patent-search services. Patent records describe applications and grants; a patent's existence is not proof that an invention works or is legally valid in every country.
Limit to websitesEnter domains separated by commas, for example nature.com, arxiv.org, nih.gov. Acadia keeps results whose indexed publisher, repository, or PDF links match those hosts. It filters returned metadata; it does not crawl the entire site.
Publication yearLimits results to the selected range recorded by an index. Items with missing dates may not appear inside a year-filtered search.
Minimum citationsShows works that meet the selected reported citation count. The count can be delayed, incomplete, or different elsewhere.
Open access only / Has abstractRestricts records to those where available metadata marks an open-access copy or supplies an abstract. Missing metadata is not proof that no copy or abstract exists.
SortRelevance follows index ranking; Newest first sorts by indexed year; Most cited sorts by the count available to Acadia.
Advanced searchCan limit to an exact phrase, author, publication, title-only matches, excluded terms, or a document type when the index supports that filter.

Where information comes from

OpenAlex provides broad records for works, authors, journals, institutions, citations, and access links. Crossref provides DOI registration and publisher-deposited metadata. Semantic Scholar supplies scholarly graph records, abstracts, citation links, and some full-text links. The indexes overlap, and each has gaps or delays.

Saved papers, search history, and saved alerts live in your browser's local storage. They are not synchronized to an Acadia account. Clearing browser data can erase them. The patent and case-law options currently open specialist sites directly rather than showing those sites' records as Acadia-hosted results.

Use more than one signal. Read the abstract and methods, check the publication date and version, follow the DOI to the original record, look for corrections or retractions, and compare important claims with independent research. Citation counts, source colors, and source-context meters cannot tell you on their own whether a claim is true.
